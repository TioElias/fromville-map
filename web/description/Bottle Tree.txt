### **Overview**
Discovered by Sheriff Boyd Stevens and Sara Myers during their high-stakes expedition into the deep woods, this specific Bottle Tree stands far beyond the town's explored parameters. While it visually resembles the Farway Bottle Tree used by Tabitha, it does not function as a physical teleportation gateway. Instead, this ancient tree acts as a historical time capsule for Fromville, decorated with dangling glass bottles that contain handwritten chronological evidence of the town's centuries-old cycle.

---

### **Key Features**

* **The Clinking Branches:** Dozens of glass bottles hung by strings from the branches, which sway in the wind and create a distinct clinking sound that guides travelers to the clearing.
* **The Cursive Artifacts:** Unlike the digits found in the gateway tree, the notes inside these bottles contain specific years meticulously written out in cursive.
* **The Spider Territory Border:** This tree marks the immediate physical boundary where the deep forest begins to transition into the territory of massive, unseen arachnid entities.

---

### **Notable Lore (Season 1)**

* **The Edge of the Map:** As Boyd and Sara trek deeper into the forest than any resident has ever dared to go, they follow a mysterious clinking noise to this clearing. Sara is stunned to find the tree, noting that Boyd himself previously stated that this territory was completely unreachable.
* **Sara's Seizure:** The moment Boyd reaches up to knock one of the glass bottles down to inspect it, a massive wave of supernatural feedback hits the clearing. Sara experiences an excruciating, violent seizure and collapses to the ground, forcing Boyd to abandon his investigation to tend to her.
* **The 1864 Reveal:** After setting up their tent nearby to let Sara recover overnight, Boyd reveals that he successfully retrieved a slip of paper from one of the fallen bottles. The note reads exactly **"1864"**, dropping the first major historical confirmation to the survivors that previous groups of travelers have been trapped in the Township for centuries.

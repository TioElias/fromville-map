Overview
Located in a neutral clearing between the Town and the forest, this large, flat rock is where newly arrived survivors must make a permanent, defining choice. After spending their first night surviving a monster attack, newcomers are brought here by Sheriff Boyd Stevens and Colony House leader Donna Raines to choose how they want to live out their entrapment: under the laws and structures of the Town, or the communal, free-spirited isolation of Colony House.

Key Features
The Two Baskets: During the ceremony, two distinct baskets are placed on the stone. One contains a traditional Talisman (representing the Town's focus on individual family units and rules), and the other contains white flowers (representing Colony House's philosophy of shared resources and communal living).

The Ultimatum: The choice made at the stone is absolute. Once a resident selects a path, they are expected to stay in their designated zone, drawing a harsh cultural line through the middle of the community.

The Audience: Ceremonies are typically attended by residents from both sides, who watch from a distance to see who will join their respective factions, creating a high-stakes, tense atmosphere for the terrified newcomers.

Notable Lore
The Matthews Family Split (Season 1): The ceremony became a major flashpoint when the Matthews family arrived. While Jim, Tabitha, and Ethan chose the Town, their teenage daughter, Julie, rebelled and chose Colony House. This fractured the family immediately upon their arrival, forcing them to live under completely different survival conditions.

Ideological Battleground: The stone highlights the deep philosophical rift between Boyd (who believes in order, routine, and finding a way home) and Donna (who believes the town is a permanent trap and that survival requires living for the present moment without rigid rules).

A Rite of Passage: The ceremony is designed to force newcomers to accept reality. By making them choose a survival method, Boyd and Donna strip away the denial of being "lost" and force them to face the rules of the environment.

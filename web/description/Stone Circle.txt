### **Overview**
The Stone Circle is an ancient, deeply unsettling megalithic structure hidden on the outskirts of the Township. It consists of seven large, weathered boulders heavily smeared with an eerie reddish-brown pigment resembling dried blood. Surrounded by primitive, hand-crafted scarecrow totems and stick figures, this landmark completely predates the modern Town. It acts as a major physical manifestation of Fromville's dark history, operating as a localized focal point for spiritual energy and visions.

---

### **Key Features**

* **The Seven Red Boulders:** Massive rocks arranged in a precise circle. The number and orientation of the stones exactly mirror the seven stone slabs found in the deep sacrificial chambers of the subterranean Tunnels.
* **The Scarecrow Perimeter:** Grotesque, life-sized figures fashioned from twigs, burlap, and twine that stand guard around the stone clearing.
* **The Flattened Center:** A perfectly cleared patch of ground inside the ring that remains unaffected by the surrounding forest overgrowth.

---

### **Notable Lore**

* **Father Khatri’s Sanctuary (Season 1):** Long before the town's peace shattered, Father Khatri frequented this clearing to pray, meditate, and contemplate the impossible nature of their reality. He famously marveled at the absolute anomaly of the location, questioning how an ancient European-style pagan structure could physically exist in the middle of a secluded American forest. 
* **The Psychic Conduit:** The circle possesses a unique connection to the entities running the Town. It is near this daylight clearing that Father Khatri buried a bag containing a dark secret from his past life—a secret that the forest's malevolent voices were instantly able to detect and manifest through Sara Myers' drawings.
* **Miranda's Paintings (Season 3):** The deeper, generational significance of the site is unmasked through the rediscovered artwork of Victor's mother, Miranda. Decades before the current timeline, Miranda had extensively painted this exact Stone Circle, linking its imagery directly to the spirits of the "Anghkooey" children who are anchored to the stones.
